# Jam at the standard of a company that raised a hundred million

> **Status:** decided 2026-09-13 (night) by the owner: "what's stopping us
> from implementing all of this? If nothing, launch workers." Wave A is
> building on branch `jam-v4` from `jam-v3`; contract in
> `plans/tasks/jam-v4/BRIEF.md`. Wave B waits for the owner's first session
> with Wave A. This document is the plan; the passes before it are
> `JAM_MODE.md` (the feature), `JAM_UX_DECISIONS.md` (the screen),
> `JAM_SOUND.md` (the drums).

## 0. The verdict this answers

Asked whether Jam, sounds aside, is at the standard of a product that is
winning its market, the honest answer on 2026-09-13 was no. What is at
that standard: the engine (sample-accurate, deterministic, probe-gated,
five hundred tests), the design process, fifteen languages. What is not,
in the order a paying musician notices:

1. **The band loops; it does not play.** No intro, no build across
   choruses, no breakdown, no ending. One bar with a fill every N.
2. **Half the band is synthesised.** Bass and keys are the sines the drums
   were.
3. **Content is thin.** Nine vibes, twenty-five grooves, six starter jams,
   no way to bring your own song in.
4. **The band does not listen.** The mic exists; the jam ignores it.
5. **The coach is absent from the room.**
6. **No moment of delight.** Nothing in the first minute makes someone
   show a friend.

## 1. The bar

A guitarist opens the app, taps Rock, and within five seconds is playing
over something that sounds like three people in a rehearsal room who have
played together before: the drummer starts with a pickup, holds back on
the first chorus, opens up on the second, drops to the hats for a bar or
two when the form comes round, and ends the tune when it is time. The bass
walks where the style walks and sits where it sits. The keys comp, not
pad. When the guitarist pastes the chords of a song they are learning, the
band plays that song. When they stop, one line tells them something true
about what they just played and one thing to try. Nothing they hear
sounds programmed; nothing they touch takes a manual.

## 2. The stages

### A1 — The band plays a song (the arrangement engine) · Wave A · W30 + W29
A jam gains an **arrangement**: `loop` (today), `build` (default) and
`song`. Build: a pickup fill into bar one; chorus one held back (soft,
closed hats, sparse bass); chorus two normal; chorus three and after
loud, open hats where the style opens them; every fourth chorus a
breakdown chorus (drums to kick and hats, bass alone for the first half,
keys out); stop-time on the last bar of a section where the style stops
(blues, rock); a crash at the top of every chorus; fills at section seams
scaled to the dynamics. Song: build, for a chosen number of choruses,
with an ending (a held chord and a cymbal, or a stop) after the last. All
of it computed in TypeScript per bar from the chorus number and the form,
sent bar-ahead as today, with two small engine additions: a table that
asks to be applied **at the bar line** (drums included, so the next bar's
drummer does not arrive a bar early) and a bar that **ends the form**.

### A2 — Sound complete (recorded bass and keys) · Wave A · W33 + W29
Recorded, public domain (Karoryfer Samples, all CC0): fingered and picked
electric bass from Black And Blue Basses, upright from Meatbass, slap from
whichever free Karoryfer bass carries a slap articulation, and the Rhodes
from jRhodes3 (CC0, 5 layers, 16 MB) for the electric piano. Rendered by
the drum render tool grown for melodic instruments into note banks the
engine builds pitches from at load. Organ, clavinet, pad and synth bass
stay synthesised: those are synthesis instruments in real life, and the
existing recipes are honest for them. A level match across the band
through the same bus.

### A3 — Content at scale · Wave A · W31
Grooves from twenty-five to about one hundred and twenty, authored per
style family against the reference cards, never generated by formula.
Fifty curated starter jams across the nine vibes, each with a real
progression and a name a musician would use. **Paste a chord chart**: a
text box that takes `| Am | F | C | G |`, `Am7 D7 | Gmaj7 |`, iReal-style
lines or a lyric sheet with chords above the words, and makes a jam of it
with the key guessed and the form set to the chart's length. The jam
library gets style filters.

### A4 — Delight · Wave A · W32
Every vibe tile plays two bars when you hover or hold it, on the real
engine. A **Jam now** button on the empty Jam screen and in the rail's
Jam entry that picks a vibe for your instrument and starts the band in
one tap. The first-run hints for Jam rewritten to three that matter.

### A5 — The percussionist · after Wave A · W35 + W36 + W37
Decided 2026-09-14 after the owner's first session with Wave A ("it's so
usable right now… if we add anything let's just do that for now"): one
more layer, a percussionist, and nothing else before the polish pass and
the ship. Ten voices from Virtuosity Drums' auxiliary percussion (public
domain): shaker, tambourine, cowbell, cabasa, claves, guiro, two congas,
two bongos, as a kit-format set played under every drum kit; a
Percussion row in the band with its own mute and level; parts authored
per groove where a percussionist belongs, on by default for latin, funk,
pop and world. Contract in `plans/tasks/jam-v5/BRIEF.md`. Rhythm guitar
and horns wait.

### On sharing presets
The owner, 2026-09-14: preset sharing must be a built-in marketplace of
sorts, browse and take inside the app; import and export files are too
much friction to be the main path. Recorded here so no pass proposes
files as the answer; it is a later stage with its own plan.

### B1 — The band that listens · Wave B · W34
Tempo follow (the band nudges toward the player's pulse within a window,
off by default); dynamics follow (when the player drops to a whisper the
drummer follows); tap a chord on the timeline while playing to change it
for this chorus. All from the input the app already has.

### B2 — The coach in the room · Wave B · W35
After a jam, not during: one honest sentence per section about where the
player sat against the chord tones and the time, and one thing to try
next chorus. The owner's brief from day one: "centred on the flow of the
jam, like a person in the room." No score, no exam. Built on `coach.rs`
and the take pipeline.

## 3. The order, and why

A1 and A2 are what make it a band; A3 and A4 are what make it a product;
they share no files and run in parallel. B1 and B2 change how a jam
*feels* and need the owner to have felt Wave A first, so they wait one
session. Sound stage A2 depends on downloads the owner approves per file.

## 5. What stays true

The five Jam principles hold: the band never plays your instrument;
everything the app plays is deterministic; the band is a table on the tick
grid, never a second clock; thirty seconds to playing; say what the score
is worth. The click stays sacred. Nothing is downloaded at runtime.
Nothing leaves the machine.
