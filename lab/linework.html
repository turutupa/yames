Technical linework — three concepts

Edges only: 3D geometry rendered as line art, no fill, no lights, no post-processing. Drag the sliders. Everything is procedural, so any of it can be re-posed, re-coloured or exploded further.

Concept 1

The Exploded Metronome

Case, movement plate, gear train, escape wheel, anchor, pendulum rod and sliding weight, bell. Drifts apart along its own axis; the rod swings at the tempo.

Concept 2

The Escapement

The mechanism that actually makes the tick: escape wheel and anchor. It steps one tooth per beat — the wheel only moves when the anchor lets it.

Concept 1b — the coach variant

The Metronome That Notices

+0 ms IN THE POCKET

The same machine, with one idea added: the rod is drawn twice. The solid one is the beat. The dashed one is where the player actually landed, and it lags behind. The gap between them is the drag — annotated in milliseconds off a graduated arc, the way a technical drawing calls out a tolerance.

Concept 3

The Practice Rig

Laptop, floating widget, footswitch and MIDI pedal, exploded along the desk. Says "this fits your setup" without needing a photograph.

Rough: proportions and detail are first-pass. The point is to judge the treatment. Line weight, density of detail and how far things explode are all cheap to change — that is the tuning that separates this from looking like a wireframe.
